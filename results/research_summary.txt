AAPL Black-Scholes Implied Volatility Analysis
Research Summary


1. Research Question
====================

This project investigates the implied volatility structure of AAPL call options under the Black-Scholes framework.

The main research question is:

Does the constant volatility assumption of the Black-Scholes model
adequately describe the observed market implied volatility pattern?


2. Dataset
==========

Underlying asset:
Apple Inc. (AAPL)

Quote date:
2020-12-31

Expiration date:
2021-02-19

Option type:
Call options

Sample size:
10 option contracts


The research sample contains options with different strike prices
but the same quote date and maturity.

This design allows the analysis of the cross-sectional relationship
between strike price and implied volatility.


3. Methodology
==============

The project implements a European Black-Scholes call option model.

Option prices are calculated using:

- underlying price
- strike price
- time to maturity
- risk-free interest rate
- dividend yield
- volatility parameter


Because volatility is not directly observable,
implied volatility is obtained by solving:

Black-Scholes theoretical price = Market option price


The numerical inversion is performed using Brent's root-finding method.


4. Model Assumptions
====================

Risk-free rate:

r = 0.001


Dividend yield:

q = 0.0


Time to maturity:

T = DTE / 365


The model represents European-style call options.

Listed AAPL options are generally American-style,
therefore the calculated IV should be interpreted
under the simplified Black-Scholes assumptions.


5. Results
==========

All 10 option contracts successfully produced implied volatility values.


Calculated implied volatility range:

Minimum:
38.35%

Maximum:
45.51%


The implied volatility decreased from approximately:

45.51% at strike price 110

to:

38.35% at strike price 140


and increased again toward:

40.67% at strike price 155.


This produces a volatility smile pattern,
showing that market prices do not follow
the constant volatility assumption of the basic Black-Scholes model.


6. Validation
==============

Each calculated implied volatility was substituted back into
the Black-Scholes model.

Validation results:

Successful repricing:
10 / 10


Maximum absolute pricing error:

0.0000000000


Mean absolute pricing error:

0.0000000000


This confirms numerical consistency of the implied volatility solver.


7. Comparison With Provided IV
==============================

The calculated implied volatility was compared with the
C_IV field provided in the dataset.


The largest difference was approximately:

1.47 percentage points


at strike price:

110


The difference decreased near at-the-money strikes.


Possible explanations include:

- different option pricing assumptions,
- dividend treatment,
- interest rate assumptions,
- quote construction methodology,
- American versus European option differences.


8. Limitations
==============

This analysis has several limitations.


First, the Black-Scholes model assumes European exercise,
while listed AAPL options are American-style.


Second, the analysis assumes zero dividend yield,
although AAPL pays dividends.


Third, the risk-free rate is simplified rather than matched
exactly to the historical market environment.


Finally, the conclusion is based on a single quote date
and one maturity with 10 option contracts.

The results should therefore be interpreted as
a case study rather than a universal description
of AAPL option markets.


9. Future Extensions
====================

Possible improvements include:

- incorporating historical dividend yields,
- using maturity-matched Treasury rates,
- comparing European and American option models,
- analysing multiple expiration dates,
- constructing a full volatility surface,
- performing volatility parameter calibration.
